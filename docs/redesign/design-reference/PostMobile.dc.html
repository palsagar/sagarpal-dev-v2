Gray–Scott in 200 lines of WGSL

A reaction–diffusion solver written as WebGPU compute shaders, and what ping-pong buffers teach you about GPU memory.

12 Oct 202614 min read2,140 reads

Reaction–diffusion systems are the rare corner of physics where two lines of PDE produce something that looks alive. The Gray–Scott model has two species, u and v, feeding and consuming each other on a grid.

J. E. Pearson mapped the (F, k) plane into a dozen pattern families in “Complex Patterns in a Simple System”, Science 261 (1993).

The model

The reaction turns u into v at rate uv2, fresh u is fed in at rate F, and v is removed at rate F + k:

∂u∂t = Du ∇2u − uv2 + F(1 − u)

Ping-pong buffers

A dispatch can't safely read and write the same texture, so the state lives in two. Each step reads one and writes the other.

let r  = s.x * s.y * s.y;
let du = P.du * lap.x - r + P.f * (1.0 - s.x);
let dv = P.dv * lap.y + r - (P.f + P.k) * s.y;
textureStore(dst, p, vec4(s + P.dt * vec2(du, dv), 0.0, 1.0));
